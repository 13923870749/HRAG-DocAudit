\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We evaluate \HRAG{} under a dual-track protocol. The public track (C3PA, ContractNLI) runs Tier-1 replication on official splits using a deterministic hybrid-retrieval proxy; the private track reports Tier-2 deployment metrics from a CNAS-accredited laboratory LLM pipeline, with Tier-2b hold-out replication validating the temporal split only.

\subsection{Experimental setup}

Table~\ref{tab:datasets} lists the three corpora. C3PA \cite{c3pa2024} links privacy-policy segments to 12 CCPA mandates; ContractNLI \cite{koreeda2021contractnli} provides 607 NDAs with 17 hypotheses each; CNAS is a 2023--2024 partner-laboratory corpus of 87 reports and 523 checklist items labeled by senior auditors.

We compare Rule-Only, RAG-Only (hybrid retrieval, $\lambda=0.6$, document-scoped chunks), Ensemble (OR fusion when a rule signal fires), and \HRAG{} (priority cascade). Self-RAG \cite{asai2023selfrag} and ReAct \cite{yao2023react} are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:tier2_transfer} under the C3PA Tier-1 proxy; they were omitted from CNAS Tier-2 because the partner pipeline did not authorize additional multi-step LLM calls on private reports. Reported metrics include accuracy, macro-F1, lenient hallucination (unsupported positives without valid evidence, excluding rule-routed outputs), evidence hit rate, NDCG@10, and engine conflict rate; strict hallucination (any false positive) appears in \ref{app:hallucination}. McNemar tests compare \HRAG{} against RAG-only on both public tracks ($p<0.01$). On both test splits, \HRAG{} and OR-ensemble produce identical predictions ($p{=}1$).

\subsection{Public-track evaluation (Tier-1)}

Table~\ref{tab:public} and Figure~\ref{fig:public_benchmarks} summarize official test-split results. On C3PA, \HRAG{} matches OR-ensemble on accuracy and eliminates lenient hallucination relative to both OR-ensemble and RAG-only (McNemar vs.\ RAG-only, $p<10^{-36}$); strict false-positive rates for \HRAG{} and OR-ensemble are identical (\ref{app:hallucination}). On ContractNLI, \HRAG{} improves over RAG-only ($p<10^{-12}$) and ties OR-ensemble on accuracy, although OR-ensemble achieves lower overall lenient hallucination by fusing RAG on semantic-only items ($n{=}1681$). On rule-amenable subsets, priority routing sharply raises accuracy over RAG-only on both datasets (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism_evidence}a).

\subsection{Private-track deployment (Tier-2)}

Figure~\ref{fig:cnas_deployment} and Table~\ref{tab:cnas} report partner-laboratory deployment metrics for Rule, RAG, Ensemble, and \HRAG{}---the configurations executed on the 87 CNAS reports. These results are not reproduced by the Tier-1 proxy; Tier-2b hold-out accuracy reaches 65.3\%. Manual review of 45 misclassified hold-out reports attributed most errors to \textbf{temporal concept drift} (72\%): 2024 reports adopted the revised CNAS-CL01:2023 clause on ``digital report appendices'', absent from 2022--2023 training material. The rule engine missed the new fields, and the RAG retriever failed to surface updated standard passages because of lexical mismatch. Static rule--RAG pipelines are therefore vulnerable to regulatory version change; cascade routing is best viewed as a controlled gating pattern under stable rule coverage rather than as a temporally invariant auditor. On the in-distribution CNAS deployment set, \HRAG{} reaches 94.3\% accuracy, 4.2\% lenient hallucination, and 8.5\% HITL load---lower human review than RAG-only (18.2\%) while meeting the laboratory's operational review-load target more closely than ensemble fusion.

\subsection{Ablations and mechanism analysis}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} ablates CNAS deployment components. Removing priority routing or the rule engine increases both HITL load and lenient hallucination. On Tier-1 data, rule--RAG conflict remains high globally (45.2\% on C3PA; 54.4\% on ContractNLI) and on routed items (46.0\%/32.3\%), yet \HRAG{} removes lenient unsupported positives where OR-ensemble does not (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism_evidence}b). Validation NDCG@10 peaks at $\lambda=0.9$ on both public tracks (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism_evidence}c). We retain $\lambda=0.6$ as the default because ContractNLI validation RAG accuracy is unchanged between $\lambda=0.6$ and $\lambda=0.9$ (55.93\%, $n{=}1037$; Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism_evidence}d), so the ranking peak does not improve classification. $\lambda$ was selected on the ContractNLI validation split (English contracts); a dedicated grid on the 20-report CNAS validation hold-out was not feasible under partner API limits. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} nevertheless shows that dense-only retrieval ($\lambda{=}1$) lowers CNAS accuracy from 94.3\% to 91.2\%, supporting a hybrid default over pure dense retrieval. The optimal weight for Chinese technical reports may differ from 0.6; CNAS-specific tuning remains an open empirical question.
