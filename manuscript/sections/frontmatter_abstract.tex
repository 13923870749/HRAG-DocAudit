\begin{abstract}
Accredited testing laboratories must audit conformity assessment documents under strict traceability and bounded human-review load. In China National Accreditation Service for Conformity Assessment (CNAS) accredited settings, manual review remains slow, inconsistent, and difficult to scale when standards change. Pure retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with large language models (LLMs) improves semantic coverage but can still produce unsupported claims, whereas the rule engine is auditable yet incomplete for semantic checklist items. We propose Hybrid Retrieval-Augmented Generation (HRAG)-DocAudit, a priority-cascaded framework that routes keyword-triggered items to deterministic rules and delegates remaining items to document-scoped hybrid retrieval and LLM judgment, with confidence-tiered human-in-the-loop (HITL) escalation at deployment. Evaluation combines open Tier-1 replication on C3PA and ContractNLI with Tier-2 deployment on a partner-laboratory CNAS corpus. On C3PA, cascade routing raises rule-amenable accuracy from 50.6\% to 92.5\% over RAG-only. The cascade eliminates RAG-origin unsupported positives on rule-routed items (lenient metric), while maintaining comparable strict false-positive rates to ensemble methods, with the added benefit of auditable rule traces and lower HITL load. McNemar tests against RAG-only are significant on both public tracks ($p<0.01$). CNAS deployment on 523 checklist items reaches 94.3\% accuracy, 4.2\% lenient hallucination, and 8.5\% human review load, compared with 87.4\%, 14.9\%, and 18.2\% for RAG-only. The Tier-1 replication code, synthetic data generators, and preprocessed C3PA/ContractNLI splits are publicly available at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/HRAG-DocAudit-D8AE} (anonymized for review). The full Tier-2 pipeline code is proprietary to the partner laboratory but remains available under an academic license upon publication.
\end{abstract}
